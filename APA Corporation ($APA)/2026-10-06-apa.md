# APA Corporation ($APA) — Public Company Analysis
**Analysis Date:** 2026-10-06  
**Analysis Period:** Q3 2025 – Q2 2026 (trailing four reported quarters; Q3 2026 results not yet reported as of the analysis date)  
**Primary Data Source:** Official Investor Relations & SEC Filings

## 1. Executive Summary & Positive Milestones

APA's last four reported quarters show a company that has used a strong 2026 oil price environment to compound operational outperformance in the Permian, cut costs ahead of schedule, and pay down debt, while building long-dated optionality in Suriname, Alaska and Uruguay. The offsets are falling reported volumes (Egypt, North Sea, U.S. gas/NGL) and a heavy dependence on oil prices for the step-up in earnings and cash flow.

- **Q3 2025**: Production beat in every operating area, and Egypt de-risked - Management stated production exceeded guidance in each operating area with capex and operating costs below guidance, and that Egypt past-due receivables were nearly eliminated after substantial payments, alongside a significant Egypt acreage award. GAAP net income was $205M ($0.57/share); adjusted net income was $332M ($0.93/share). Net debt was $4.01B at 9/30/25. *(Q3 2025 earnings call; Q2 2026 release net-debt table)*
- **Q4 / FY2025 (reported 2026-02-25)**: Cost program hit target two years early - $350M of run-rate controllable spend savings achieved by year-end 2025, with the target raised to $450M for year-end 2026. FY2025 operating cash flow was $4.5B, free cash flow $1.0B, adjusted EBITDAX $5.4B; $640M (>60% of FCF) was returned to shareholders. Year-end proved reserves grew 9% to 1,056 MMBOE (734 MMBOE proved developed). Permian inventory assessment validated ~10 years of economic inventory. Q4 U.S. oil production was 132 Mb/d. Year-end net debt was $3.98B (total debt $4.49B). *(Q4 2025 release, 8-K 2026-02-25)*
- **Q1 2026 (reported 2026-05-06)**: GAAP net income attributable to common of $446M ($1.26/share); adjusted earnings $489M ($1.38/share). U.S. oil production was 124.0 Mb/d; full-year U.S. oil outlook raised to 122 Mb/d with Permian capital unchanged at $1.3B. Free cash flow was $477M and adjusted EBITDAX $1.56B. $634M of near-term bonds repaid through April 2026. *(Q1 2026 release, 8-K 2026-05-06)*
- **June 2026**: Alaska infrastructure acquisition - Agreement to acquire Savant Alaska, LLC for ~$70M upfront plus contingent payments tied to future development. Includes the Badami facility (~40 Mb/d nameplate), the Nutaaq Pipeline (~80 Mb/d, connecting to TAPS), ~17,000 net acres in the Badami unit (~1,500 b/d current production), and takes APA's eastern North Slope position to ~487,000 gross acres. Closing expected by year-end 2026, subject to regulatory approval. *(IR release 2026-06-10/11; Q2 2026 release)*
- **Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-05)**: Beat-and-raise quarter - Adjusted earnings of $669M ($1.89/share) vs. $313M ($0.87) a year earlier; GAAP net income attributable to common of $747M ($2.11/share) vs. $603M ($1.67). U.S. oil production of 123.5 Mb/d was 2.5 Mb/d above guidance; full-year U.S. oil outlook raised to 123 Mb/d at unchanged $1.3B capital; LOE guidance cut $25M to $1.5B; 2026 exit run-rate savings target raised to $500M from $450M. Free cash flow was $738M ($1.2B in H1). *(Q2 2026 release, 8-K 2026-08-05)*
- **Q2 2026 balance sheet**: Net debt fell to $3.30B from $3.98B at year-end 2025; $752M of near-term bonds repaid in H1 (including $673M in Q2); total debt is down $2.3B since year-end 2024, cutting annualized interest expense by more than $155M. *(Q2 2026 release)*
- **Q2 2026 exploration**: Eni partnership in Uruguay - APA signed Eni S.p.A. as strategic partner in offshore Block 6 (OFF-6); APA keeps a 60% working interest and Eni funds most of the initial exploration well planned for 2027. *(Q2 2026 release)*
- **Egypt gas**: Gross gas production reached 539 MMcf/d in Q2 2026 (vs. 479 in Q2 2025), and nearly half of Egypt gas now benefits from the revised pricing agreement. *(Q2 2026 release, production tables)*

## 4. Bullish Scenario (Upside Thesis)

- **Catalyst 1: Permian efficiency compounding.** APA has now beaten U.S. oil guidance repeatedly (Q3 2025, Q4 2025, Q1 2026, Q2 2026) while running fewer rigs and holding capital at $1.3B. If it holds ~123 Mb/d on four rigs, with ~10 years of validated inventory and a $3.5M/month Permian opex-savings target, oil FCF per dollar of capital keeps rising.
- **Catalyst 2: Deleveraging into equity value.** Net debt has fallen ~$680M in six months to $3.3B, and the company has taken out $2.3B of total debt since year-end 2024. With adjusted EBITDAX of $3.4B in H1 alone, leverage is well under 1x annualized. Lower interest (>$155M/yr savings) and the 60%+ FCF return pledge create room for a larger buyback and dividend. The H1 payout was only ~23% of FCF, so there is a catch-up opportunity.
- **Catalyst 3: Cost reset is structural.** $350M of savings was achieved two years early, and the target was raised twice (to $450M, then $500M). LOE guidance has been cut while volumes were beaten.
- **Catalyst 4: Egypt gas pricing and receivables.** Nearly half of Egypt gas now earns the revised price, past-due receivables were nearly eliminated in Q3 2025, and gross gas volumes are growing (539 MMcf/d, up from 479 a year ago).
- **Catalyst 5: Long-dated growth optionality.** GranMorgu (Suriname) is the main organic oil growth driver from mid-2028; Savant gives Alaska infrastructure at a small upfront price; Eni's funding of most of the OFF-6 well in Uruguay reduces APA's exploration cost.
- **Target Drivers:** Sustained oil near current levels; Egypt volumes stabilizing as gas development offsets oil/cost-recovery declines; buybacks accelerating in H2 as net debt approaches a stated comfortable level; on-time, on-budget GranMorgu progress; an exploration success in Suriname, Alaska or Uruguay that re-rates the resource base.

## 5. Bearish Scenario (Downside Thesis)

- **Risk Factor 1: Earnings are largely a price story.** Realized U.S. oil rose from $64.84 to $98.46 year over year, and adjusted EPS more than doubled while adjusted production fell 12%. Q1 2026 U.S. oil realization was only $72.53, so the Q2 level may not persist. A reversion toward 2025 prices would pressure FCF, the buyback pace and the deleveraging timeline. APA also had a $105M net derivative loss in H1 2026, which shows hedging can cost upside.
- **Risk Factor 2: Shrinking volumes outside the Permian.** Adjusted production fell 12% YoY and 5% QoQ. Egypt adjusted volumes dropped 14% QoQ (secondary sources attribute part of this to PSC cost-recovery mechanics, where higher prices reduce cost-recovery barrels), the North Sea is down 30% YoY with minimal investment and rising decommissioning spend, and U.S. gas is down 22% with *negative* realized gas prices. Management trimmed the Egypt gross gas outlook (Khafre deferral).
- **Risk Factor 3: Concentration and counterparty/geopolitical exposure.** Egypt contributes a large share of cash flow and is subject to government payment, currency and fiscal-term risk, and one-third of Egypt economics flows to Sinopec (NCI distributions of $164M in H1). The Permian drives most adjusted volumes.
- **Risk Factor 4: Capital-intensive, unproven growth.** GranMorgu (first oil mid-2028, operator-controlled) carries deepwater cost and timing risk; Alaska and Uruguay are frontier exploration with dry-hole risk (Q2 dry-hole expense was $43M). Savant adds a regulatory-approval condition and open-ended contingent payments.
- **Risk Factor 5: Legacy liabilities.** A $677M decommissioning contingency for sold Gulf of America properties, North Sea abandonment spend ($55M of abandonment and decommissioning in Q2), and $234M of current taxes in Q2 weigh on conversion of EBITDAX to FCF.
- **Risk Factor 6: Cash-flow quality and capital-return pacing.** H1 returned only ~23% of FCF versus a 60%+ target; the Q2 cash flow benefit from working capital means H1 FCF may overstate the run-rate.
- **Downside Drivers:** WTI/Brent falling back toward 2025 levels; another quarter of Egypt volume or gas-outlook cuts; Permian well results that miss the efficiency gains while rigs are cut; GranMorgu delays or cost overruns; Egyptian payment arrears reappearing; no meaningful H2 step-up in shareholder returns.
