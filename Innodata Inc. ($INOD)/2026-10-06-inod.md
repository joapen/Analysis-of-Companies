## 1. Executive Summary & Positive Milestones
- **Feb 26, 2026 (Q4/FY2025)**: Record 2025, initial 2026 guide - FY2025 revenue of $251.7M (+48% organic); Q4 revenue $72.4M (+22%); FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA $57.9M (+68%). Management initiated 2026 guidance of ~35% or more revenue growth. Form 10-K filed Feb 26, 2026.
- **Q1 2026 (reported May 7)**: Beat-and-raise - revenue $90.1M (+54% YoY); Adjusted EBITDA $25.0M (28% of revenue, vs. $12.7M); Adjusted Gross Margin 47%; GAAP net income $14.9M ($0.42 diluted). Guidance raised to ~40% or more growth (from ~35% or more, set ten weeks earlier).
- **May 7, 2026**: New Big Tech engagements - a set of engagements with a Big Tech company expected to generate ~$51M of 2026 revenue; revenue from this customer was zero twelve months earlier. Other Big Tech customers in aggregate grew 453% YoY in Q1.
- **Q1 2026**: Evaluation and Observability Platform launched in beta (control plane for agentic systems); first platform engagement (~$1M revenue, hyperscaler) closed shortly after launch; management said 15 other companies were evaluating it and that it was in channel-partnership discussions with two hyperscalers.
- **Q1 2026**: Balance sheet and credit - Wells Fargo facility renewed and expanded from $30M to $50M on a three-year term, undrawn; no appreciable debt. Also moved to single-segment reporting (previously DDS, Agility, Synodex).
- **Q1 2026**: Research credibility - one Innodata researcher had two papers accepted at ICML 2026, one with "Spotlight" designation (~2% of ~24,000 submissions per the release).
- **Aug 4, 2026**: Product release - first stage of the AI Cyber Training Suite (twelve datasets and evaluation systems to train AI coding agents to write and repair secure code).
- **Q2 2026 (reported Aug 6)**: Record quarter, 12th consecutive quarter of YoY growth - revenue $92.1M (+58%); Adjusted EBITDA $25.4M (27.5% of revenue, +92%); Adjusted Gross Margin 49% (vs. publicly stated 40% target); GAAP net income $14.4M ($0.41 diluted, vs. $0.20). Management said it beat consensus on all key metrics (consensus figures are management's characterization, not independently verified).
- **Q2 2026**: Diversification delivered - largest customer fell to 37% of revenue (from 56% in Q1); the Big Tech customer announced in Q1 rose to 34% (from 17%), becoming #2. A new customer described as one of the fastest-scaling frontier labs was added.
- **Q2 2026**: Agentic RL positioning - new program with the largest customer on personalization of long-horizon agents, plus a second program on RL environments for computer-use agentic tasks; two public benchmarks released; egocentric data-collection pilots with leading robotics companies.
- **Aug 6, 2026**: Reiterated FY2026 guidance of ~40% or more growth; management stated that likely large programs from new and existing customers are not in that figure.

## 4. Bullish Scenario (Upside Thesis)
- **Catalyst 1 - Customer diversification plus conservative guidance:** Two Big Tech accounts now total ~71% of Q2 revenue, but the mix is shifting and the base is widening (new frontier lab, other Big Tech aggregate +453% in Q1). Using the disclosed percentages, the #2 customer contributed roughly $15M in Q1 and ~$31M in Q2, i.e. ~$47M in H1 against the ~$51M the company said it "could" generate for the full year (my arithmetic from rounded percentages). If that run-rate holds, that account alone is tracking well above the original estimate. With H1 already at $182M, the 40% floor looks beatable if H2 simply holds the H1 run-rate (~$364M annualized, ~45% growth).
- **Catalyst 2 - Mix-driven margin expansion with operating leverage:** Adjusted gross margin of 49% is nine points above the 40% target; management attributes it to off-the-shelf datasets (IP retained and resold across customers) and high-value pre-training programs. Adjusted EBITDA margin has been ~27-28% for two straight quarters. Pre-training capability (built with ~$1.3M investment per the Q3 2025 call) generated ~$68M of signed and expected programs mostly flowing in 2026, evidence that R&D spend converts to revenue within quarters.
- **Catalyst 3 - New frontiers: agentic RL, evaluation platform, cyber, robotics, federal/enterprise:** The Evaluation and Observability Platform, RL-environment programs, AI Cyber Training Suite and egocentric robotics data are early-stage options on higher-quality, potentially recurring revenue. Executive Chairman Abuhoff is explicitly redeploying his time to federal and enterprise.
- **Catalyst 4 - Balance-sheet optionality:** ~$134M of cash net of prepayments, an undrawn $50M facility, an ATM shelf and a CFO with an M&A background give capacity for acquisitions or capacity build-out.
- **Target Drivers:** (1) Large unannounced programs get signed and added to guidance (a raise above 40%); (2) customer #1 revenue re-accelerates in H2 as promised; (3) adjusted gross margin holds at or above 45%; (4) platform converts evaluations into recurring contracts; (5) a smooth CEO transition on Sep 30 with no customer disruption.

## 5. Bearish Scenario (Downside Thesis)
- **Risk Factor 1 - Extreme customer concentration and lumpy, at-will programs:** Q2 revenue from two customers was ~71%. The largest customer fell from 56% to 37% of revenue; applying those percentages to reported revenue implies a ~$16M sequential decline (~$50M to ~$34M, my calculation) that management attributed to a program structure and service mix change. In the Q4 2025 call (secondary source), management cited post-training workflows from the largest customer being deprecated, representing ~$20M of annualized run rate. The company's own risk language notes that customers can reduce, delay or cancel projects on short notice and that contracts are primarily at-will.
- **Risk Factor 2 - Dilution and capital-markets signal:** A $300M ATM filed the day of a record quarter, with $250M of reported cash (~$134M net of prepayments), invites questions about use of proceeds and dilution. Third-party market data placed market cap near $1.9B around mid-2026, which would make a fully used ATM material (roughly 15%); treat that ratio as indicative only. Diluted share count was already ~34.8M in Q2.
- **Risk Factor 3 - Earnings quality and cost growth:** GAAP selling and administrative expense grew ~88% YoY in Q2 (vs. revenue +58%), and SBC grew ~164%. Adjusted EBITDA excludes SBC, flattering the profit trend. H1 cash flow was inflated by customer advances and payables tied to pass-through costs, which will unwind; the release does not break out how pass-through revenue and cost affect reported revenue or margins, so I could not verify how much of reported growth is low-margin pass-through (check the 10-Q revenue recognition note).
- **Risk Factor 4 - Leadership transition and competition:** A new CFO (since Jul 6) and a new CEO (from Sep 30) overlap with the fastest scaling phase in the company's history. The AI data-services market is competitive and customers can in-source or switch vendors; the company's own filings cite emerging and growing competitors among its risk factors.
- **Risk Factor 5 - Sentiment and positioning (third-party, context only):** A Sep 22, 2026 Hunterbrook Capital report reportedly named Meta as Innodata's top customer, and the stock was reported up ~15% that day, with short interest cited around 14%. The company has not (in IR materials I reviewed) named its customers; treat this as unverified and as evidence of the stock's headline sensitivity.
- **Downside Drivers:** Q3 revenue below the ~$85M/quarter implied guidance floor; further sequential decline at the largest customer without offsetting #2 growth; adjusted gross margin falling back toward the 40% target as dataset-licensing mix normalizes; heavy ATM issuance without a clear acquisition or growth use; any customer loss or insourcing among the top two; operating cash flow reversing as prepayments are consumed.
