## 1. Executive Summary

Qualcomm is a fabless semiconductor leader with two engines: QCT (chips, ~80% of revenue) and QTL (wireless-standards licensing, ~19%, near-100% gross margin, an earnings floor). The IOS factor screen (data 2026-09-10) ranks it #7 of 47 with a Composite of 68.0 and Forecast +12.9 — Core bucket, trend "Up" (50DMA > 200DMA), no earnings-quality flag. Fresh data pulled today complicates the picture: price ($180.79–181.03) is now sitting almost exactly on its 200DMA ($180.26) and *below* both its 50DMA ($187.88) and 100DMA ($189.10) — a short-term downtrend inside a still-positive long-term structure. Over the past year QCOM returned only +7.2% while the semiconductor sector (SOXX) returned +111% and the S&P 500 returned roughly +14% YTD — QCOM has been a severe laggard in an AI-fueled sector rally, which is the central tension in this name: cheap and diversifying, or cheap for a reason.

## 2. Thesis

- **QTL is a royalty floor, not a chip bet.** ~130,000+ wireless-standard patents mean Qualcomm collects royalties on essentially every 3G/4G/5G handset sold globally, independent of who wins the chip socket — near-100% gross margin, structurally defensive.
- **Diversification away from handsets is real and accelerating.** Automotive revenue +59% YoY (Q2 FY26) against a disclosed $45B design-win pipeline; management's target is $22B of non-handset revenue by 2029 (from ~$10B now) — automotive, IoT (Arduino acquisition), PC compute (Snapdragon X Elite/Oryon), and a new AI200 data-center chip with a hyperscaler customer expected H2 2026 (reinforced by the Alphawave acquisition).
- **Valuation is undemanding for the quality.** 21.0x trailing / 19.5x forward earnings, 5.4% FCF yield, 25.6% ROIC, 33.8% ROE, 54% gross margin — a high-quality balance sheet (Debt/Equity 0.55, current ratio 2.02) priced like a cyclical, not like the AI-infrastructure story management is trying to tell.
- **FY2025's ugly net-income print was a one-off, not operating decay.** The $5.7B non-cash tax charge from the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (OBBBA) — not disclosed to or flagged by the IOS Screen Flags tab, since the TTM figures have already recovered past it — masked record FY2025 revenue of $44.3B (+14%) and FCF growth (+14.9%). TTM net income has already rebounded to $9.26B.
- **Momentum, within the IOS universe, still ranks it top-10** (Mom Score high, Trend "Up") even though in absolute/market terms it has lagged badly — the stock may simply not have caught the AI re-rating yet.

## 3. Anti-thesis

- **It has been a value trap for a full year.** +7.2% over 12 months against SOXX's +111% is not noise — the market has had a full AI-capex supercycle to re-rate Qualcomm and chosen not to, which is itself information.
- **Apple modem risk is real and sized.** ~20% of QCT exposure; the supply agreement runs through 2027, and a successful Apple transition to its own modem would remove an estimated $7–8B of chip revenue (though QTL royalties persist regardless).
- **China concentration (~60% of revenue) plus an antitrust probe opened October 2025** is a live regulatory tail risk in a worsening US-China environment — not merely theoretical, and not something a factor screen prices in.
- **Data-center execution is unproven.** Qualcomm tried and failed to scale server silicon once before (Centriq 2400, 2017). AI200 vs. Nvidia's CUDA moat is a software-ecosystem problem as much as a silicon one; "a large hyperscaler customer" is not yet revenue.
- **Handset cyclicality hasn't gone away** — FY2023 revenue fell 19%; the diversification story is real but still a minority of the business.
- **Technically, the stock just broke its 50- and 100-day averages to the downside and is testing the 200DMA from above.** Barchart's composite read is "Strong Sell" (RSI ~36, MACD negative). The long-term "Up" trend label is accurate but is sitting at its most fragile point — a close below $180 would put QCOM in a confirmed downtrend on all three horizons.
- **Bear case from external research: $140–160 target** (vs. $181 now) if Apple exits, data-center underwhelms, and China tightens — a ~20–25% further drawdown scenario, consistent with compressing back to ~14x on depressed earnings.

## 12. Learning Opportunities

- Concrete illustration of **why momentum should be measured market-relative, not just universe-relative** — QCOM's IOS Mom Score flatters it versus 46 other names, but says nothing about its standing versus SOXX or SPY. Consider adding a true relative-strength-vs-benchmark column to the factor screen.
- Good case study for the **Earnings-Quality Flags framework's blind spot**: a real one-time distortion (OBBBA tax charge) existed in FY2025 but doesn't show up as a flag once TTM data rolls past it — the flag logic is point-in-time and can miss a charge that's already faded from the trailing window.
