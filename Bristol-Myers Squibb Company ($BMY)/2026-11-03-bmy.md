## 1. Executive Summary

BMY is the IOS S&P 500 screen's #7-ranked name (Composite 82.7, Carver target weight ≈2.6%, Core Compounder bucket on low beta + solid quality metrics). The thesis is a classic "cash-flow compounder trading like a melting ice cube": Eliquis is still growing double digits in the U.S. even as international patent losses bite, the legacy generics cliff (Revlimid, Pomalyst) has already done most of its damage, and the market is pricing BMY at roughly half the multiple of its pharma peers on a forward basis.

## 2. Thesis

- Bristol Myers grew sales on US blood-thinner pricing and newer drugs while licensing deals cut into quarterly profit. Q3 2026 revenue was $13.1 billion, up 7% from the prior-year period, with Eliquis worldwide revenue up 25% to $4.69 billion.
- Bristol Myers raised its full-year sales outlook by $500 million at the middle of the range, attributed to "broad-based and continuing momentum across the portfolio."
- Growth-portfolio drugs beyond Eliquis are contributing: Breyanzi's revenue increased 143% year over year to $224 million in Q3, and iza-bren cut breast cancer progression risk by up to 38% in a Phase 3 trial. Four late-stage trial wins and an on-time FDA approval for multiple-myeloma pill Zenbexus landed in the same window.
- Despite this, the stock trades at roughly 10x forward earnings — the cheapest among its 11 S&P 500 pharma peers — for a company guided to $46-47.5B+ in annual revenue with a 4%+ dividend yield and a growing newer-drug franchise.

## 3. Anti-thesis

- The patent cliff is not hypothetical — it is already in the numbers. Revlimid revenue fell nearly $1 billion YTD after full generic competition began Jan 31, 2026; the Legacy Portfolio (Revlimid, Pomalyst, Sprycel) shrank 55% YoY to $725 million in Q3, with generics taking 47% of Revlimid sales and 81% of Pomalyst sales.
- The next leg is already on the calendar: Q4 2026 total revenue is guided roughly flat to down (-0.3% YoY) as European Eliquis patents expire in November 2026.
- Eliquis (US exclusivity to 2029 at the latest) and Opdivo (cliff near 2028) together are roughly 45% of revenue — concentration risk in two aging franchises.
- Sentiment is cooling even on beat-and-raise prints: Q3 earnings fell 17.2% YoY, Cobenfy's launch has stalled, margins dipped, and much of the quarter's upside came from Eliquis specifically — the drug with the clearest known cliff.
- Any setback across the ~12 registrational data events anticipated in 2026 would hit a company that is leaning hard on new assets to offset expiring patents.

## 8. Risks

- **Patent cliff concentration:** Eliquis + Opdivo are ~45% of revenue, both facing exclusivity loss by 2028-2029; EU Eliquis losses are already live and hitting Q4 2026 guidance.
- **Earnings-quality noise:** Recurring acquired-IPR&D/licensing charges are depressing GAAP EPS quarter to quarter, making trailing P/E unreliable — a textbook IOS **"Charge?"** flag pattern (high/volatile trailing P/E readings vs. a stable, much lower forward P/E). Verify against the live screen row before trusting any single trailing-P/E print.
- **Pipeline execution risk:** The bull case leans on Cobenfy, Breyanzi, Camzyos, milvexian and the CELMoD platform landing on schedule; Cobenfy's launch has already "stalled" per sell-side commentary.
- **Leverage:** Debt/equity around 2.9x and total debt in the $44-46B range — manageable for this scale of cash flow, but not negligible.
- **Policy risk:** Medicare price-negotiation pressure specifically named on Eliquis pricing.
